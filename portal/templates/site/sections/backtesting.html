Backtesting

Backtesting with the losing trades left in

Its purpose is to examine how clearly defined rules behaved against historical data under stated assumptions — not to look perfect.

What to examine in a backtest

Number of trades

Very small samples can give misleading results.

Winning and losing trades

A strategy should be examined across both.

Drawdown

Study the decline between equity peaks instead of looking only at profit.

Profit factor

Compare aggregate profitable trades with aggregate losing trades.

Costs

Spreads, commissions and other assumptions can materially change a historical simulation.

Time period

A strategy tested during only one type of market environment may behave very differently later.

Backtests are not forecasts

Historical simulations cannot predict future results. They are research tools. Use them to understand strategy behaviour — not as a guarantee of what a live account will produce.
