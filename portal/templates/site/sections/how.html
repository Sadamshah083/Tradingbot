How Sambot trading automation works

The objective is not to remove decision-making. It is to automate a defined set of rules consistently while keeping the process visible.

01

TradingView creates the signal

A supported TradingView strategy generates an alert when its configured conditions are met. The alert contains the information Sambot needs to identify the supported market and intended action.

02

The webhook receives the alert

Your Sambot account provides a dedicated webhook endpoint. TradingView delivers the alert to that endpoint when the configured condition triggers.

03

Sambot validates it

Before anything continues, Sambot checks the incoming signal against its supported configuration. Invalid, unsupported or duplicate signals can be rejected instead of being silently processed.

04

Your risk settings are evaluated

Sambot applies the account’s configured limits — risk per trade, maximum open trades, exposure, daily loss thresholds and losing-streak controls.

05

Paper or supported connected execution

In paper mode, Sambot records a simulated fill. For a supported connected setup, the validated workflow can be routed through the configured integration.

06

Position-management rules run

Depending on the configuration, Sambot can track stop-loss, take-profit, break-even and trailing-stop rules.

07

Everything is recorded

Signals, rejections, order activity and completed workflow events are available for review. Automation should be inspectable.

Start in paper mode

Before evaluating any live automation, first confirm:

  • signals are arriving correctly;
  • the correct market is being identified;
  • position sizing behaves as expected;
  • protective rules appear correctly;
  • rejected signals show the correct reason; and
  • your strategy behaves differently in real-time data than expected.
